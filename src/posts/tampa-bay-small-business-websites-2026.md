Between November 16 and 23, 2026, we took one quick, read-only look at the home page of every Tampa Bay business website we could find in open map data: 6,587 sites across Hillsborough, Pinellas and Pasco counties, Lakeland and Plant City. We wanted to know where local businesses actually stand, not what a national survey says.

No business is named here. Every number below is a total across many businesses.

## The short version

- **Most sites get the basics right.** 92% of the sites that loaded were secure (HTTPS) and set up for phone screens.
- **1 in 4 had no description for search results**, so Google writes its own summary of the page.
- **About 1 in 5 sites with images had at least one image with no text description**, which is what screen readers read out loud to blind and low-vision visitors.
- **About 6% of the website links in business listings went nowhere useful:** a dead address, a parked "for sale" domain, or a placeholder page. Another 2.6% pointed at a page that no longer exists.

None of these is a big project. Most are a few minutes in your website editor or your listing settings.

## Good news first

Of the 4,117 sites that loaded, 92% did both of the things browsers care about most: they loaded over HTTPS (the padlock), and they had the setting that tells phones how to size the page.

Only 2.9% didn't work over HTTPS, and 4.6% weren't set up for phone screens. Whoever built and hosts these sites has mostly kept up.

## The most common gap: no description for search results

A quarter of the sites (24.9%) had no meta description, the one or two sentences a page gives search engines to show under its title. Without one, Google picks some text from the page itself, and it may be a cookie notice or a list of menu links.

**How to check:** search Google for your business name and read the two lines under your website's title. If they don't say what you do, where, and for whom, it's worth writing your own.

**How to fix:** most website builders have a "SEO description" or "search description" field in each page's settings. Write one or two plain sentences, the way you'd describe your business to a new customer.

## Images without text descriptions

About 1 in 5 sites with images (21.7%) had at least one image with no text description, also called alt text. A screen reader, which blind and low-vision visitors use to browse, reads that description out loud. Without one, they hear nothing, or a file name like "IMG_4021.jpg".

**How to check:** in your website editor, click a photo and look for a field called "alt text", "image description" or "accessibility text".

**How to fix:** describe what the photo shows in a few words ("Our dining room on a Friday night", "Dr. Lee with a patient"). Purely decorative images, like a background pattern, can be marked as decorative instead.

A note on this one: we counted it from each page's code as it was delivered. A few sites add descriptions later with JavaScript, which this count can't see. When we re-checked 15 of these sites in a full browser, it agreed on 14.

## The website link on your listings

This one surprised us. The businesses in this study came from open map data (OpenStreetMap and Overture Maps), the kind of listing data many maps and apps draw on. For about 6% of them, the website listed went nowhere useful:

- 146 didn't lead to any website (the domain no longer points anywhere)
- 213 were parked domains, often with a "this domain is for sale" page
- 46 showed a web host's placeholder or a "coming soon" page

Another 168 (2.6%) pointed at a page that no longer exists, usually an old link to a specific page after a site was rebuilt.

And for 461 more, the page at the listed address never mentioned the business, so it may have changed hands, or the listing may point at a different business with a similar name. We didn't count those in the 6%, because a page can mention a business in ways our check can't read.

**How to check:** look up your business on Google Maps, Apple Maps, Yelp and Facebook, and tap the website link on each. Does it open your site, on the right page?

**How to fix:** update the link in each listing's settings. If you changed domains, keep the old one for a while and have it forward to the new one.

## The smaller things

- **Form fields with no label** (7.4% of sites): a contact form whose boxes have nothing in the page's code saying what goes in them. A screen reader then says just "edit text". Each field needs a label, which can be visually hidden if the design needs it.
- **Slow to answer** (2.8%): the server took more than three seconds to start sending the page. That's before any images load.
- **No page title** (0.9%): the title shows in the browser tab, and usually as your headline in Google. It's rare, but it's a one-line fix.

Accountants and print shops were the most likely to be missing a search description, and dental offices and home-service companies the least. The differences are small, though. These gaps are spread across every kind of business, not stuck to one.

## A 10-minute check of your own site

1. **Open your site on your phone.** Is the text readable without zooming? Do the buttons work with a thumb?
2. **Type your address with https:// in front.** Does it open your site with a padlock, not a warning?
3. **Search Google for your business name.** Do the two lines under your site say what you'd want them to say?
4. **Tap the website link on your Google, Apple Maps, Yelp and Facebook listings.** Does each one open your site?
5. **Try your contact form.** Fill it in and send it to yourself. Did it arrive?

If all five pass, you're ahead of most. If one doesn't, it's usually a small fix.

## How we checked

- **One page per business:** the home page (or the page its listing links to), loaded once, read-only, between November 16 and 23, 2026. Nothing was clicked, typed or submitted. Our checker identified itself as MobiusProspector and followed each site's robots.txt.
- **What counts:** a site "loaded" if it answered with a real page. Of the 6,587 sites, 4,117 did. Many of the rest turned our automated check away (bot protection, a firewall, a timeout), which says nothing about how they work for people, so we left them out of every percentage above except the listing numbers, which only count clear cases.
- **Not a random sample.** It covers businesses whose websites are listed in OpenStreetMap and Overture Maps, and it leans toward law, insurance and accounting offices, which we looked at more closely. Chains and franchises are mostly left out.
- **Automated checks have limits.** They find many problems but not all, and a page can look worse (or better) to a script than to a person. Treat these numbers as a picture of the region, not a grade for any one business.
